The short answer

What questions expose a weak sustainability claim?

Ask what exactly is claimed, which product or component it covers, the evidence owner, geography, verification source, date, operating boundary, measured result, exclusion, reviewer and next recheck. If those fields are absent, narrow the claim.

What this cannot decide

The checklist does not certify or endorse a product.

01

Copy the exact claim before judging it

Small qualifiers change meaning. A bottle made with some recycled plastic, a package designed for recycling and a product called plastic-free are three different statements that require different evidence.

Save the exact words, page or panel, date and product variant. This prevents a later review from testing a broader or narrower claim than the customer actually saw.

02

Ask who verified what, and for how long

Evidence should identify the issuer, legal holder, product or component scope and validity period. Self-declaration can still provide useful operational detail, but it should not be presented as independent certification.

Database verification is strongest when the product name and designation match the brand's wording. A company-level policy, ingredient certificate and finished-product certificate should remain distinct in the review record.

03

Look for exclusions and practical instructions

A claim becomes more useful when it states where it does not apply. Mixed-material pumps, limited collection pin codes and an uncertified product variant are material facts, not footnotes to hide.

Consumer action should also be feasible. If a packaging claim requires separation, a take-back request or an industrial facility, the instructions and access route belong beside the claim.
