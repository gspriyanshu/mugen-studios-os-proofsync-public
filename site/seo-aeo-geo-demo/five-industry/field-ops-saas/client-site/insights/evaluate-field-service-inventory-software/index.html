The short answer

What should an inventory-focused software evaluation include?

Use your own locations, event vocabulary, offline needs, integrations, roles and exception cases as the demo script. Score evidence against weighted criteria instead of accepting a generic feature checkmark.

What this cannot decide

This framework does not rank vendors or confirm their current features.

01

Write the inventory scenario before watching the demo

A feature checklist makes most products look equivalent. A scenario exposes the handoffs: reserve a part at one branch, issue it to a technician, substitute it during the job, return the unused unit and explain every balance afterward.

Use the same scenario with every vendor and ask the demonstrator to show the event history rather than describe what is possible. Record configuration, integration or plan dependencies next to each observed step.

02

Weight requirements by operational consequence

Offline capture may be essential for a rural service fleet and irrelevant for an always-connected indoor team. Serial tracking may be mandatory for high-value equipment but unnecessary for common consumables.

Weights should be approved before scoring so a polished interface cannot erase a hard operational gap. Separate must-pass conditions from preferences, and document who owns each judgment.

03

Evaluate implementation and control, not only screens

The product must fit the systems that already own customer, financial, purchasing and asset data. Ask which records synchronize, how failures surface, how history is preserved and who resolves conflicting edits.

A strong selection record includes migration assumptions, role permissions, count and correction workflow, support model and exit/export options. Price comparison without those operating costs is incomplete.
