The short answer

How should a service team set van-stock reorder points?

Start with demand history, service criticality, replenishment lead time, storage limits and the cost of a missed job. Set an initial min/max rule, then review exceptions instead of treating the first number as permanent.

What this cannot decide

The framework supplies no benchmark or guaranteed stock level for a real trade or fleet.

01

Start with demand during replenishment lead time

A reorder point should answer one narrow question: how much stock can be consumed before the next reliable replenishment arrives? The inputs are recent item demand, replenishment lead time, delivery cadence and an explicit buffer for variability.

The arithmetic is less important than the definitions. Teams should decide whether emergency transfers, substituted parts and cancelled jobs count as demand, then apply the same rule across comparable vehicles before comparing results.

02

Segment truck stock by service consequence

Fast-moving, low-cost items may justify a higher buffer because a shortage stops common work. Expensive, rarely used or highly specialized parts may belong at a branch with a documented transfer path instead of occupying every vehicle.

Criticality is not the same as price. A small connector can be operationally critical, while a high-value assembly may be pre-assigned only after diagnosis. The item policy should record both service consequence and replenishment option.

03

Review exceptions before raising every minimum

Repeated stockouts can indicate an undersized minimum, but they can also reveal missed consumption, inaccurate van balances or an unreliable replenishment run. Raising the threshold without diagnosing the exception may convert a data problem into excess stock.

A useful review compares recommended quantity, counted quantity, recorded quantity, shortage history and returns. Changes should be time-bound and reviewed after a defined number of replenishment cycles rather than treated as permanent after one difficult week.
