The short answer

Why does most grid-tied solar shut down during an outage?

Grid-tied systems normally stop exporting during an outage to protect workers and equipment. Supplying home loads requires a compatible design that isolates from the grid and manages selected loads, commonly with storage and approved control equipment.

What this cannot decide

This page cannot promise backup capability or runtime for any real system.

01

Start with the safety shutdown rule

A conventional grid-tied rooftop system generally does not keep powering the home when the grid fails. The system must stop energising utility lines unless it has compatible equipment that can safely isolate the home and establish a local source.

That means panels on the roof do not by themselves prove outage capability. The answer depends on inverter functions, storage, transfer/islanding equipment, protected-load design and the commissioned system configuration.

02

Define the loads before sizing backup

Backup design begins with the circuits the homeowner needs during an outage, their running demand and startup surges. Refrigeration, communications, medical needs, well pumps, air conditioning and EV charging create very different system requirements.

Battery capacity cannot be translated into a fixed runtime without the load profile, state of charge, reserve settings, temperature and solar conditions. Publish ranges only when a qualified designer has documented the assumptions.

03

Test the complete operating sequence

A resilient system needs more than equipment labels. The homeowner should understand how the system detects an outage, which panel or circuits remain live, whether solar can recharge storage while isolated, and how the home returns to grid service.

Commissioning records, homeowner training and periodic testing are part of the outcome. The fictional simulation can show the questions but cannot claim an installed system, certified design or emergency-service capability.
