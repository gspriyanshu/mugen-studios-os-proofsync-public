The short answer

What roof questions should come before a solar quote?

Review roof age and condition, leak history, remaining life, structure, shade, usable area, orientation, electrical service, planned reroofing and the contract terms for removing and reinstalling equipment later.

What this cannot decide

Only qualified roof, structural and electrical assessment can decide whether a real home is ready.

01

Inspect roof condition before panel layout

A proposal drawing does not establish that a roof is ready for decades of mounted equipment. The homeowner should understand roof age, leak history, remaining service life, material, prior repairs and whether a roofer or structural professional recommends work before installation.

If reroofing is likely, the analysis should include the future cost and responsibility for panel removal and reinstallation. The solar contract and roof warranty should be reviewed together so one scope does not silently undermine the other.

02

Map shade, structure and electrical constraints

Trees, neighbouring structures, roof planes and equipment obstructions can change the usable solar area and production model. A site-specific shade assessment should identify its method and assumptions instead of relying on a satellite image alone.

The service panel, meter, grounding, rapid-shutdown design and structural attachment also require qualified review. The article must not diagnose these conditions from homeowner photos or imply that the fictional client employs licensed professionals.

03

Confirm jurisdiction and project dependencies

City of Austin permit information is relevant only when the property and project fall under the applicable jurisdiction. Homes in an extraterritorial jurisdiction or another municipality may follow a different permitting path even when the postal address says Austin.

Record roof work, tree decisions, electrical upgrades, homeowner-association review, permit and utility interconnection as separate dependencies. A credible proposal explains which party owns each task and what can change the schedule or price.
