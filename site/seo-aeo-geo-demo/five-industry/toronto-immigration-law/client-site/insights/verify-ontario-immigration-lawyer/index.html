The short answer

How can someone verify an Ontario immigration lawyer?

Search the live Law Society of Ontario directory for the person's name, check practising status and business details, compare them with the retainer and payment instructions, and clarify scope, fees, privacy and responsibility in writing.

What this cannot decide

The checklist verifies public records and consistency; it does not endorse or rank a professional.

01

Match the person to the LSO directory record

The LSO directory lists Ontario lawyers and paralegals and displays practising status, business contact information and, where applicable, regulatory history or restrictions. Search the individual's name and compare the result with the website, email domain, invoice and proposed retainer before sending identity documents or money.

A logo, badge, search result or social profile is not a substitute for the regulator's record. If names or business details do not align, pause and contact the professional through independently verified information. The directory check confirms regulatory information; it does not endorse the person or establish suitability for a particular case.

02

Understand who IRCC recognizes as a paid representative

IRCC says a person does not need to hire a representative and that using one does not improve the chance of approval. It also states that paid representatives must be authorized, including lawyers and paralegals in good standing with a Canadian provincial or territorial law society and other listed professional categories.

Ask the proposed representative to identify the regulator and provide a written scope of work. Confirm who will perform the work, how communications will be handled and which fees or third-party charges apply. This checklist does not determine competence or create a lawyer-client relationship.

03

Treat guarantees and unverifiable credentials as warning signs

IRCC expressly warns that representation does not determine an application result, while LSO rules require lawyer marketing to be demonstrably true, accurate, verifiable and non-misleading. Claims promising a certain outcome, implying special government access or asserting an unsupported universal win record should prompt further verification rather than confidence.

Keep copies of the proposed retainer, fee explanation, receipts and written communications. Do not send original identity documents through an unverified channel. If a person needs help locating a legal professional, the LSO Referral Service is an official route, but a referral is not an outcome promise or endorsement.
