Dental Content Brief Template: Question, Answer, Evidence, Reviewer and Limit

A pre-draft contract that turns a keyword opportunity into one useful page with accountable evidence, review ownership and explicit stop lines.

Direct answerA dental content brief should define one reader question, the direct answer the page must earn, the evidence required for material claims, the person responsible for review and the limits the page must not cross. Recording those decisions before drafting reduces duplication, unsupported health language, revision loops and accidental publication of unreviewed claims.

Key takeaways

  • A keyword list describes possible demand; a brief grants controlled publication work.
  • Write the provisional direct answer before commissioning the full article.
  • Map material claims to named sources and reviewers.
  • “Clinical review pending” is a valid hold state; an invented reviewer is not.

Why a keyword list is not enough

A keyword list says what people may search. It does not decide what the page should answer, where evidence comes from or when marketing language becomes a health claim. Those unresolved decisions create duplicate articles and expensive review loops.

Begin with the intent clusters from dental keyword research, but authorize a page only after it receives a distinct reader job, source plan and claims boundary.

The Q-A-E-R-L brief

Question

Name the primary reader, exact question, decision stage and desired next action.

Answer

Set the page thesis, provisional direct answer and original decision tool.

Evidence

Attach official or otherwise authoritative sources to each material external claim.

Reviewer

Assign real editorial, SEO, claims, clinical and release authority where applicable.

Limit

Record prohibited claims, jurisdictional questions and escalation triggers.

Question: define one job

“What must a real clinic prove on a service page?” gives an editor a clear owner-level task. “Dental SEO” does not. Record neighbouring pages so the new brief does not duplicate the service-page checklist or another page’s responsibility.

Answer: state the editorial contract

Write a 40–70 word provisional answer. Add the named framework, three to five decisions the reader should make, one practical example and one limitation. If the team cannot state a useful conclusion, the topic is probably too broad, speculative or already owned.

Evidence: map, do not decorate

For each material claim, record the source URL, source owner, date checked, supported wording, jurisdiction and refresh trigger. A citation list added after drafting cannot repair a source that never supported the sentence. Google’s people-first guidance rewards original value and clear authorship rather than lightly rewritten source material.

Reviewer and limit: make authority explicit

Name the writer, editorial reviewer, SEO/AEO/GEO reviewer, claims reviewer and final publisher. Add a qualified clinician only when a real, verified person accepts the review. Patient diagnosis, treatment suitability, medication, emergency guidance, recovery, prognosis and outcome promises are hard-stop triggers for this owner-facing workflow.

Copy-ready dental content brief

Page identity and ownership

  • Working title, final H1, slug and content owner.
  • Primary reader, reader question, search intent and next action.
  • Primary topic, secondary terms and pages this must not duplicate.
  • Unique thesis, named framework, required internal links and inbound-link targets.

Answer and evidence requirements

  • Provisional direct answer and required decisions.
  • Original example, limitation and owner-appropriate CTA.
  • Claim, source, authority, date checked and citation location.
  • Jurisdiction, reviewer requirement and refresh trigger.

Review and release fields

  • Writer, editorial reviewer, SEO/AEO/GEO reviewer and claims reviewer.
  • Clinical reviewer when genuinely required and available.
  • Current status: draft, revise, approved, held or published.
  • Baseline date, reporting window, measurement source and missing-data rule.

Prohibited territory

  • Patient-specific advice or symptom triage.
  • Procedure suitability, medication or emergency instructions.
  • Recovery, prognosis or guaranteed outcomes.
  • Unverified practitioner, clinic, service, price or insurance facts.
  • Jurisdiction-specific claims without the appropriate review.

Practical owner example

Question: What must a genuine dental service page prove?

Answer contract: The page must show a verified service, responsible clinic facts, accountable review, a clear administrative next step and limitations consistent with real operations.

Evidence: Google’s people-first guidance, applicable professional advertising rules and the clinic’s own approved operating records.

Limit: Treatment names can illustrate page architecture, but the article cannot decide who should receive treatment. Any patient-health explanation moves to the clinical-review workflow.

Brief approval gate

  1. One reader and question are named.
  2. The answer does not duplicate another page.
  3. Material claims have sources or an explicit pending state.
  4. Review ownership is real.
  5. Prohibited territory and escalation are recorded.
  6. The success measure has a source and time window.

A disciplined brief saves time because it finds unresolved decisions before they become paragraphs. It also gives the TRACE answer system a stable production input.

Scope and limits. This template routes review; it does not approve clinical content. No clinical status is valid without an identifiable qualified reviewer. Applicable professional advertising and legal requirements must be checked in the clinic’s jurisdiction.
